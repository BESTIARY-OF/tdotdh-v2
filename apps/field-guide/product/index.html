01 / TREE

What is actually high-dimensional?

Every node is a partial field: which pieces have been placed, their translations, the order they arrived, the remaining frontier, and the centroid-adjacent contact geometry. Two nodes can share a family seed while having completely different outer coastlines.

centroid→contact seed→partial field→complete arrangement
02 / BEAM

What the search keeps

The worker grows connected candidates around the selected centroid. It retains a bounded beam, branches only from the current frontier, rejects holes, and reports progress by depth. Breadth mode reserves beam space for distinct unseen family seeds.

03 / HINTS

What saved directions do

Direction hints are optional steering, separate from family exploration. A strict edge-extension test assigns each piece one dominant cardinal direction. When enabled, completed candidates with more matching hints rank ahead of otherwise valid candidates.

04 / PROJECTION

How to read the Atlas

The Atlas places observed families by the number of shapes directly touching the centroid and their best coastline. The marker contains the exact centroid-adjacent thumbnail and family ID. This is a useful phase-space projection, but it does not include rejected nodes that were never captured.

seed familymarker size = arrangementsvertical position = coastline

Loose finite bounds: The selected centroid exposes a finite set of orthogonal exterior slots. The current worker’s 24 × 16 field cap also gives a deliberately loose outer-piece placement bound before overlap, connectivity, hole, and packing constraints.
